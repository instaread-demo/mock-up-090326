Road freight figures point to productivity growth

by Transport News | Sep 2, 2026 | Features

The first quarter of 2026 suggests there was a marked uplift in productivity, according to the latest road freight figures published by the Department for Transport (DfT).

Between January and March 2026, UK-registered HGVs lifted an estimated 387 million tonnes of goods, 8% more than the 360 million tonnes recorded in the same quarter of 2025.

The amount of freight moved, which takes weight and distance into account, also increased by approximately 8%, from 39 billion to 42 billion tonne-km.

At the start of 2026, UK productivity growth was supported by a bounce-back in manufacturing, steady expansion in the services sector, and a more accurate alignment between gross value added (GVA) and hours worked, according to the Office for National Statistics.

Freight and logistics consultant John Robertson said: “This is an encouraging indication that the road freight sector has started 2026 with greater momentum.

“The increase suggests operators are moving more goods with their existing assets, pointing to stronger utilisation and improving efficiency across the sector.”

Wider DfT data covering the 12 months to March 2026 points to a stable rate of productivity in domestic HGV operations, with UK-registered HGVs operating domestically lifting an estimated 1.56 billion tonnes of goods, much the same as in the previous 12-month period ending in March 2025.

The amount of freight moved, which takes weight and distance into account, fell from 165 billion to 164 billion tonne-km, while the distance travelled by HGVs also fell from 19.1 billion km to 19 billion km.

Between April 2025 and March 2026, the data showed that empty running also remained stable. Empty vehicles accounted for 30% of HGV mileage, down from 31% in the 12 months to March 2025.

In the most recent quarter, empty running saw a substantial fall. Empty vehicles accounted for 28% of HGV mileage between January and March 2026, down from 33% in the corresponding quarter of 2025.

In absolute terms, empty running fell from approximately 1.5 billion km to just under 1.3 billion km. This represented a year-on-year reduction of approximately 200 million km.

Despite the improvement, the scale of empty running remained significant. More than one in every four kilometres travelled by a UK-registered HGV during the quarter was completed without a load.
