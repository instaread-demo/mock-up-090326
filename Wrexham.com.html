National award for Wrexham project using farming to build confidence and skills

A Wrexham community project which uses a working farm to help people build confidence and learn new skills has won a national adult learning award.

Agri-cation CIC, based on a 110-acre beef farm at Bangor-on-Dee, has won the Hywel Francis Award for Community Impact in this year’s Inspire! Adult Learning Awards

Founded by Cheryl Reeves and her husband Andrew in 2020, the organisation supports people facing barriers to employment, education and everyday life through farming, nature and outdoor learning.

Many adults attending Agri-cation courses are not in work, socially isolated or living with trauma, bereavement, long-term health conditions or low confidence.

Activities include cooking, woodwork, mindfulness, wellbeing techniques and caring for animals.

Over the past year, Cheryl and her team of three have delivered 23 courses for Groundwork North Wales, helping 232 adult learners to develop new skills.

Courses include Wellness in Wellies, supporting people experiencing isolation and anxiety, Family Farm Days for parents, carers and children during school holidays, Men/Women in Sheds and Menopause Circle.

The team is developing a new range of courses, including Lads & Dads sessions to strengthen father–son relationships, Punching Princesses to support women’s physical and mental wellbeing and Agored Cymru accredited learning opportunities.

Speaking about the award, Cheryl said: “We are delighted to win this award, which acknowledges all the work we do for the community. Hopefully, it will help to raise awareness of our business to a wider area and open the door to more people to use our facilities.”

“Each programme shares a core purpose: removing barriers, building confidence and reconnecting people with community and nature.

“What sets Agri-cation apart is that people don’t realise they are learning. By creating welcoming and supportive environments we help individuals develop skills in a way that feels nothing like formal education.”

Award nominator, Louise Stokes, training officer at Groundwork North Wales, a provider of adult community learning across Wrexham and Flintshire, said: “Agri‑cation’s impact goes far beyond skills development.

“Many learners describe the experience as transformational, reporting improved mental health, increased self‑belief, renewed motivation and a strong sense of belonging.”

Michelle Matheron, Learning and Work Institute Director for Wales, added: “Adult learning makes a real difference and it’s vital to support and celebrate adults in Wales who are embracing learning, sometimes overcoming significant personal challenges to do so. In a fast-changing world the message to “never stop learning” is more important than ever.”

The award will be presented at the Coal Exchange in Cardiff on September 23, with Agri-cation one of 11 winners.

To find out what’s going on during Adult Learners’ Week and for personalised advice on your own learning options and the support available, contact Working Wales on 0800 028 4844 or search https://workingwales.gov.wales
