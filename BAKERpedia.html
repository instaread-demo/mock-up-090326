Healthy Baking with Less Fat

The clean label trend is driving bakers to find clever new ways to provide the same great-tasting products, all with simpler labels. On one side is the consumer demand for healthier, cleaner-label products. On the other is our unwavering commitment to quality, texture, and shelf-life. The ingredient that often finds itself in the crosshairs? Shortening. It’s one of the hardest working ingredients in the bakery, but its downsides are becoming impossible to ignore.

The Essential Role of Shortening

Shortening has been a baker’s best friend for decades for a reason. It performs several vital functions across a vast array of products. In breads and rolls, even at low usage levels of 1-2%, it tenderizes the crumb and aids in dough machinability. In cakes, where usage can hit 10-20%, it’s crucial for aeration and creating that delicate, moist texture we all strive for.

However, its importance is perhaps nowhere more apparent than in tortilla production. In wheat flour tortillas, fat is essential at every stage of production. It directly impacts dough extensibility and smoothness, and ultimately, the final structure, appearance, and taste of the tortilla. However, that doesn’t come without drawbacks.

The Hidden Cost of Traditional Shortening

While shortening works wonders, it has its downsides. The first is a sensory one. Over time, fats can oxidize, leading to the development of unpleasant rancid or off-odors that can ruin a product’s appeal. The second is a business one: costs are volatile, and consumer perception is shifting towards less processed, lower-fat options.

This puts bakers in a tough spot. Reducing shortening can negatively impact your finished goods. In tortillas, for example, cutting the fat often results in smaller diameters, firmer textures, and a drier, crumblier, less pliable product. The foldability is severely compromised, leading to cracking when rolled or folded. For a producer, this also means dough that is tighter, rougher, and harder to process through machinery. Finding a way to reduce shortening without sacrificing operational efficiency or product quality is the holy grail of modern baking.

The Enzyme Solution for a Cleaner Label

So, how do we get the best of both worlds? The answer lies in the micro-world of enzymes. These powerful proteins act as highly specific catalysts, allowing us to mimic the functions of shortening without the drawbacks.

The key players here are α-amylases and proteases. α-amylases, often dubbed “liquefying enzymes,” act on starches, breaking them down into dextrins. This process can be leveraged to improve browning and, crucially, to help retain moisture, keeping the product feeling softer and fresher for longer.

On the other hand, proteases work on proteins, specifically the gluten network. A gentle breakdown of gluten enables better dough flow and reduces mixing time. This is vital for maintaining the extensibility lost when shortening is reduced. It helps restore the plasticity and machinability of the dough, ensuring it doesn’t tear or shrink during processing.

How Enzymes Transform Shortening Reduction in Tortillas

The impact of these enzymes on specific products is remarkable, especially when it comes to tortillas. Cutting shortening by 33% is a massive change, but recent studies show it’s achievable with the right enzyme solutions.

Advanced enzyme blends, like SEBake LO™ from Enzyme Innovation, are being developed to address the specific challenges of fat reduction. In tests, these blends have proven to mimic shortening function, improving the smoothness and extensibility of reduced-fat dough to match that of the full-fat control.

The results on the final product are even more compelling. Tortillas made with 33% less shortening and the enzyme blend were nearly identical in diameter and thickness to the full-fat version. More importantly, the crucial metrics of foldability and rollability were preserved. By incorporating enzymes like these, we’re not just reducing fat; we’re enhancing quality while creating a product that meets consumer demand for a healthier, more sustainable future.

Get Baking

Enzymes offer a viable, effective path to shortening reduction without compromising the quality and sensory attributes your customers love. It’s a powerful way to innovate, save on costs, and deliver a better product.
