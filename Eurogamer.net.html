Mario Kart 8 Deluxe gets a surprise Switch 2 update, bringing eight-player split-screen to the evergreen racing game

Boo!

Nintendo heard us. Our Mario Kart 8 Deluxe parties weren't simultaneously ruining enough friendships, so the evergreen racer can now be played in split-screen with up to eight players thanks to a surprise Switch 2 update.

Nintendo last night added Mario Kart 8 Deluxe to the list of games that now enjoy free Switch 2 updates. The surprise patch includes improved image quality and faster loading times when playing Mario Kart 8 on Switch 2 consoles, alongside support for GameShare and CameraPlay - meaning you can watch your soon-to-be ex-friends shout expletives as you spam them with red shells, as long as you're playing with two-to-four players.

But the headlining feature is easily the ability to play with up to eight people on the same console. Before this week, Mario Kart 8 only supported four players locally, but that limit can now be doubled. You just need to be playing on a Switch 2 and have at least one Joy-Con 2 or Nintendo Switch 2 Pro Controller connected. I'm not sure how legible Mario Kart 8 might be when the TV's sliced eight ways, but a little more chaos can't hurt, right?

A few more minor changes are also buried at the bottom of Nintendo's patch notes. Booster Course Pass owners now have a '96 Races' option when creating custom VS matches, you'll no longer be slowed down when Boo goes off to steal an enemy's item, and all racers now go a little faster "by the equivalent of one coin."

Even outside of its seemingly immortal kart racer, the Big N's been unusually busy with free updates this week. Pikmin 3 Deluxe was also treated to a free Switch 2 update just a few days ago, bringing very similar graphical improvements to the game. Meanwhile, Super Smash Bros. Ultimate cleaned up its online battles in a small patch that was pushed out earlier today. No Switch 2 update for the crossover brawler, though. At least, not yet.

Planet Coaster and Jurassic World developer Frontier partners with Disney for a new video game

My magical kingdom for a…

News by Connor Makar Reporter
Published on Sep 3, 2026, 4:53 AM EDT
1 comment
Prioritise us on Google

Frontier Developments - the developer behind Planet Coaster and Jurassic World - has announced a partnership with Disney. As part of this collaboration, a new Disney game is in production.

What exactly this game will be has not yet been announced, nor has a release window been established. All we know is it will be using Disney's IP, is fully funded by Frontier, and will "build upon the Company’s extensive experience and proven capabilities as a leader in the Creative Management Simulation (CMS) genre" according to a new press release.

Jo Cooke, CEO of Frontier, said: "We are delighted to announce this collaboration with Disney, which further reinforces our clearly defined strategy of developing and nurturing world-class creative management simulation game franchises.

"Our mission with this game is to connect a passionate creative community of players with one of the world's most iconic and beloved brands, and I can’t wait to see what our talented teams will create with this amazing opportunity."

This news comes just a week before Frontier will be releasing its quarterly earnings, during what appears to be quite the exciting time for the studio. Planet Zoo 2, scheduled to release this October, is expanded to include flying and aquatic creatures. It'll also be launching to a variety of platforms outside of PC, something its predecessor achieved gradually after its initial release.

Other than that, we know Frontier is also working on another unannounced management game, though what exactly it is is also uncertain as of writing. Its latest release, Jurassic World Evolution 3, proved quite popular among management fans and dinosaur enthusiasts alike.

What could this Disney game be? It's anyone's guess but as we're in this fun period of baseless speculation I'd love a game based around managing one of those Disney theme parks rather than a game set within the fictional settings of one Disney's many IPs. One can hope!
