New movies are coming to streaming this week and every week, at a faster pace than ever before. As always, ScreenCrush has your back, with a weekly guide to the latest releases on streaming (and digital rental or purchse) that deserve your attention. Sit back, relax, and check out what’s coming to a TV or computer (or, sigh, a phone) near you.

Then you’ve got all weekend to watch up with these titles. The theater is still the best, of course, but some weekends you just can’t muster the energy to get off the couch and haul yourself to the multiplex.

New Movies Streaming This Week and Weekend

This week you can go on a new mission with the Mandalorian, or resurrect an old spoof franchise with a new Scary Movie. You can old learn the story of Raygun (yep, that Raygun) or a unique twist on the heist film involving bomb disposal experts.

If these don’t do it for you, you can also check out some other recent streaming releases here.

Or just keep scrolling, and see the new movies you can stream or rent on digital at home this weekend.

The Mandalorian and Grogu

The first Star Wars movie is ... an adaptation of their big live-action television series. Continuing the events of The Mandalorian TV show, Pedro Pascal stars as Din Djarin, bounty hunter extraordinaire, who accepts an assignment from the New Republic (and Sigourney Weaver) to hunt down Jabba the Hutt’s kid. (Jeremy Allen White? It’s kind of a weird cast, to be honest.)

Where to watch The Mandalorian and Grogu: Streaming on Disney+.

Scary Movie

The original creative team and stars behind the Scary Movie franchise returned to lampoon the return of the original creative team and stars behind the Scream franchise a few years ago. It ain’t Shakespeare, but you do get to see Anna Faris and Regina Hall back together again, and that’s not nothing.

Where to watch Scary Movie: Streaming on Paramount+ on September 3.

Untold Raygun: Breaking Badly

Netflix’s sports doc anthology Untold brings you the tale behind one of the 2024 Olympics’ most viral superstars: “Raygun,” an Australian breakdancer whose bizarre moves (and apparently lack of qualifications to compete at the Olympics) made her the star of the Internet for a few days. At last he whole sordid tale can be told. (What a great subtitle, too.)

Where to watch Untold Raygun: Breaking Badly: Streaming on Netflix.

Fuze

Aaron Taylor-Johnson, Theo James, and Sam Worthington all star in this thriller about a bomb disposal expert (Taylor-Johnson) trying to defuse an unearthed weapon from World War II, while a group of thieves try to engineer a heist while the rest of London is distracted by the crisis.

Where to watch Fuze: Streaming on HBO Max on September 4.

Just Play Dead

Samuel L. Jackson and Eva Green as a married couple? In a comic crime thriller directed by GoldenEye and Casino Royale’s Martin Campbell? That’s Just Play Dead, where Jackson wants to fake his own death to get a massive insurance payout, but his wife (Green) might have her own plans in mind.

Where to watch Just Play Dead: Prime Video, Apple TV, Fandango.

Fall 2026 Preview: All the Movies You Have to See

Here are the fall 2026 titles you need to add to your calendar.

Sony

September 18: Resident Evil

I’ve already seen enough Resident Evil movie for a lifetime and an afterlifetime. But I will make an exception for a Zach Cregger Resident Evil movie. The man behind Barbarian and Weapons co-wrote and directed a totally new take on the franchise, with Austin Abrams as a medical courier who stumbles through a zombie apocalypse. (On the plus side, as a medical courier he should have reasonable access to replacement organs!) The trailers look like Cregger’s Resident Evil will be the stripped-down survival horror that fans of the original games have hungered for all along.

A24

September 25: Primetime

For Robert Pattinson, 2026 has been The Year of Sleazy Dudes. He played a slimy, spineless dork in The Drama, the sniveling leader of the suitors in The Odyssey, and now Chris Hansen, the host of the morally questionable NBC series To Catch a Predator. (Later this year, he’ll cap things off in Dune: Part Three. I’m guessing his character there will be another extremely upstanding and very normal guy!)

Warner Bros.

October 2: Digger

Speaking of very normal guys, Tom Cruise makes a splashy return to Acting with a capital A in Digger, from Birdman and The Revenant director Alejandro G. Iñárritu. Cruise plays a billionaire oil magnate whose business practices could bring about the end of the world. An all-star supporting cast including John Goodman, Sandra Huller, Jesse Plemons, Riz Ahmed, and Michael Stuhlbarg will back him up in a movie that looks like it could be brilliant or bizarre, but will absolutely not be boring.

Sony

October 9: The Social Reckoning

Facebook continues to generate so much controversy that they’re now earned their second Aaron Sorkin penned biographical drama. That’s quite an achievement. In The Social Reckoning (which Sorkin also directed) a whistleblower (Anora’s Mikey Madison) leaks shocking internal Facebook documents to a tech journalist (The Bear’s Jeremy Allen White), much to the chagrin of CEO Mark Zuckerberg (Jeremy Strong, replacing Jesse Eisenberg). I’m just shocked they didn’t drop the The from the title this time. It’s cleaner without it.

Neon

October 9: Fjord

2026’s top prizewinner at Cannes was this drama from Romanian New Wave auteur Cristian Mungiu about a conservative family that moves to a new village in Norway and finds itself ostrasized and even persecuted by their new neighbors. Backrooms’ Renate Reinsve and a nearly unrecognizable Sebastian Stan play the lead couple.

Paramount

October 16: Street Fighter

All you need to know about Street Fighter can be found on WWE wrestler Cody Rhodes’ enormous flattop as Guile. That tells you this latest adaptation of the long-running fighting game series is going to have some fun with the looks of these characters (also played by the likes of Noah Centineo, David Dastmalchian, 50 Cent, and Jason Momoa) and lean hard into the retro ’90s vibes associated with its most famous entry. If Street Fighter really embraces that aesthetic, it could be quite an experience.

20th Century Studios

October 16: Whalefall

Look, it’s called Whalefall and it’s about Austin Abrams getting eaten by sperm whale and then struggling to free himself from inside the stomach of the whale before he gets, y’know, eaten eaten and dies. (Apparently there are degrees of being eaten by a whale. Good to know!) If you don’t want to see that movie, there’s absolutely no hope for you.

Warner Bros.

October 23: Clayface

One of the year’s big Halloween releases in theaters is actually a DC Comics movie: Clayface, based on the Batman villain with a malleable face (and sometimes a monsterous, gloopy body) from an experimental skin treatment gone wrong. Tom Rhys Harries plays the title character, perhaps the least likely DC creation to ever get their own solo movie. But ... maybe that’s the reason it will be good? After all, you wouldn’t make a Clayface film just to capitalize on the character’s massive popularity and name recognition. He doesn’t have any! You’d only do it because you have a really great story to tell.

Toho

November 6: Godzilla Minus Zero

This fall will bring us a sequel to one of the greatest prequels ever, 2023’s Godzilla Minus One. The new film continues its predecessor’s story on the scale of both the human characters and giant fire-breathing dinosaur. Most importantly, writer/director Takashi Yamazaki returned to expand his own story and given the care he put into both the emotional arcs of the characters and the magnificent spectacle of all the kaiju destruction, Godzilla Minus Zero should be another must-see.

Neon

November 13: Paper Tiger

James Gray — the filmmaker behind titles like Two Lovers, The Immigrant, and The Lost City of Z — has long been one of America’s most interesting filmmakers, but he’s still looking for his big breakout, something that could finally come with Paper Tiger. Its period New York crime story is right in the wheelhouse of the guy who made The Yards and We Own the Night, and its cast (including Adam Driver, Scarlett Johansson, and Miles Teller) ranks among the director’s best.

Disney

November 25: Hexed

Disney’s first original animated movie in three years is a story about a teenage girl who discovers her family’s connection to a magical realm of witches. I have two daughters, and I can say with some authority: If this movie is the gene splice of a Disney princess film and Harry Potter that it looks like it is, it will be their favorite movie ever and will gross $8 billion.

Netflix

November 25: The Adventures of Cliff Booth

Quentin Tarantino still can’t settle on a tenth and (according to the arbitrary rules that he himself has set for his own career) final film. He wrote a whole sequel to Once Upon a Time in Hollywood for Brad Pitt’s laconic stunt man Cliff Booth, then decided he didn’t want to direct it. At least he allowed Pitt to use it as a means to reteam with Seven and Fight Club director David Fincher, making this a truly unique and exciting combination of creators and material.

Marvel

December 18: Avengers: Doomsday

I’m going to imagine you know enough about Avengers: Doomsday to decide whether you want to see it. I probably don’t need to summarize the plot. (What if I described it as a low-key romantic comedy set entirely in a gorgeous luxury hotel in the Swiss Alps? Would anyone believe me?) It’s pretty clear Doomsday is going to be the biggest Marvel movie ever, at least in terms of the sheer number of characters and fictional universes, including the Avengers, the Fantastic Four, the X-Men, and Robert Downey Jr.’s Doctor Doom. But will it be anything close to the best Marvel movie ever? That’s going to be $6.4 billion question.

Warner Bros.

December 18: Dune: Part Three

Yes, the two biggest movies of the fall — and maybe the entire year — both open in theaters on the exact same day. Dune: Part Three concludes director Denis Villeneuve’s trilogy of adaptations of Frank Herbert’s Dune. (Sort of; Villeneuve turned the first Dune book into two movies, this film is based on Herbert’s first sequel novel, Dune Messiah.) Much of the previous two films’ cast returns, joined now by Robert Pattison —he’s back as another bad guy! — playing the leader of a plot to eliminate Emperor of the Universe Timothée Chalamet. Anyone want to bet which of these two movies, Avengers or Dune, grosses more at the box office?

Focus

December 25: Werwulf

Christmas is one of the best days of the year to go to the movies. And what better way to make the Yuletide gay than by watching a grim new take on the classic werewolf legend from Robert Eggers, whose atmospheric period horrors include The Witch and Nosferatu? Aaron Taylor-Johnson plays the title character, while Lily-Rose Depp is his wife in this stylzed piece of ho-ho-horror.
